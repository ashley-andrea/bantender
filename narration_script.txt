[0:00 – 0:22]  Landing screen. Upload box in the middle, the bartender in the corner.

This is bantender. It takes a public tender and turns it into a priced offer
that an estimator can check, line by line. We'll use a real tender: the
ventilation works for the new Erlenstrasse primary school in Oberglatt.


[0:22 – 0:45]  Recent tenders → the tender opens on Extraction. The PDF zooms in.

The tender is already uploaded, so I open it from Recent tenders.
bantender has read all 202 pages and found 254 positions. The original PDF
is on the left, and the positions it extracted are in the middle, grouped
by system.


[0:45 – 1:21]  Click the air-handling unit. Graph appears; hover the legend, then Qty, Airflow, Make, System.

When I pick a position, a graph shows what the tender says about it:
quantity, airflow, make, system and section. The colour shows where each
value came from. Green means it was read directly, yellow means it was
derived, red means it was assumed, and grey means it's missing. Here the
quantity is red: the tender leaves it blank, so we assume one unit.
When I hover a value, the exact line in the PDF lights up. Nothing is a
black box.


[1:21 – 1:57]  Supply air disc valve → Size node → Qty node → DN100 and DN125 rows → the air-handling unit's parts.

Items that are the same product in different sizes are grouped. This supply
air valve comes in two sizes, eleven pieces in total. The size node opens
into DN100 and DN125, and the quantity splits into five and six pieces.
Each size can also be opened on its own. Accessories hang under the item
they belong to, (like the two parts of the air-handling unit.)


[1:57 – 2:23]  Catalogue. The right panel scrolls to the product graph, catalogue rows and installation rule.

In step two, every position is matched to a product. For this valve,
that's two catalogue rows: list price, our twelve percent discount, and the
net price. Below is the installation rule it uses: 0.65 hours per piece for
an installer at 96 francs an hour, (adjusted for site efficiency, mounting
height and school logistics.)


[2:23 – 3:15]  Price. Air-handling unit; hours 34 → 40; net-to-unit steps; Engineering rate 145 → 176; offer price; undo.

Step three is the price. I'll take the air-handling unit. Any value can be
corrected in place: I raise the hours from 34 to 40, and the offer updates
straight away. Below, the unit price is built in steps an estimator
recognises: catalogue net price, material waste, supplier reserve, and one
factor for project costs, overhead, risk and margin. All parameters are on
the right. I'll raise the engineering rate to 176 francs an hour… and the
offer price follows. Any change can be undone with one click, and every
edit is logged.


[3:15 – 3:21]  Click Excel.

The whole calculation exports to Excel with live formulas, (matching the
app to the centime.)


[3:21 – 3:58]  AI bid optimization. Hover the three cards, the curve, the slider, then the key drivers.

Step four goes beyond a single price. A win-probability model trained on
historical bids gives three options. Aggressive: 5.4 percent margin and a
61 percent chance of winning. Target, the recommended one: 7.7 percent
margin, 46 percent chance, and the best expected profit. Conservative:
almost 11 percent margin, but only 26 percent. With the curve I can explore
any price in between, and the key drivers show why the model says what it
says for this tender.


[3:58 – 4:31]  Choose Target → Export. Flip through the filled pages, open a system summary, scroll.

I choose Target. Step five is export. The tender requires the original
document back, so bantender writes the prices into the tender's own PDF:
648 fields on 97 pages, including totals, system summaries like this one,
discounts and VAT. The tender text is left unchanged.


[4:31 – 5:04]  Open Esteban, type "What is the submission deadline?", the answer arrives.

And this is Esteban, our assistant. You can ask him anything about the
tender, our offer or our company data. I'll ask for the submission
deadline: 22 September 2026, at ten in the morning. Every statement comes
with a source. Each answer is checked against its sources, and anything

[5:20 – 5:36]  Close Esteban, click Download PDF.

One click, and the filled-in offer is ready to submit. That's bantender:
